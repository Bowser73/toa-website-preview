If they won’t wake up, call 911.

What to do, in order

From the CDC’s overdose response guidance.

CDC: responding to an overdose
  1. Call 911Do it right away. The dispatcher can walk you through what to do.
  2. Give naloxone if you have itNaloxone (Narcan) can reverse an opioid overdose. Follow the directions on the package.
  3. Keep them awake and breathingSay their name, rub their breastbone firmly, and watch their breathing.
  4. Lay them on their sideThis helps keep them from choking.
  5. Stay until help arrivesNaloxone can wear off. Don’t leave them alone.

Signs of an opioid overdose

Someone may show one of these or several.

Pinpoint pupilsThe black centers of the eyes become very small.
Won’t wake upFalling asleep, losing consciousness, or not responding to their name or a firm rub.
Slow or no breathingBreathing is weak, very slow, or has stopped.
Choking or gurglingSnoring-like, gurgling, or choking sounds.
Limp bodyArms and legs are loose and heavy.
Cold, clammy skinSkin feels cool and damp to the touch.
Blue or gray lipsDiscolored skin, especially on the lips and fingernails.
See any of these?Call 911, give naloxone if you have it, and stay with them.

Fentanyl and fake pills

Pills bought from a friend, online, or anywhere other than a pharmacy can look exactly like real medicine and still contain fentanyl.

Talk with teens about it before it comes up. Make sure they know they can always call you for a ride, no questions asked first.

CDC: fentanyl awareness

Talk without judgment

Listen first

Ask what they’ve seen or heard, and let them finish.

Use facts, not fear

Scare tactics shut conversations down. Honest information keeps them open.

Keep the door open

One talk isn’t the talk. Come back to it.

More support

Call or text 988 for mental health and substance-use crisis support, any time.

988 does not replace emergency care. If someone may be overdosing, call 911.
